# Tropical cyclones

> Source: India Meteorological Department cyclone classification and NDMA cyclone preparedness guidance, public documentation

## Classification used in the North Indian Ocean

IMD classifies systems by maximum sustained surface wind:

- Depression: 31 to 49 km/h
- Deep depression: 50 to 61 km/h
- Cyclonic storm: 62 to 88 km/h
- Severe cyclonic storm: 89 to 117 km/h
- Very severe cyclonic storm: 118 to 166 km/h
- Extremely severe cyclonic storm: 167 to 221 km/h
- Super cyclonic storm: 222 km/h and above

## The main hazards

Storm surge, the rise of seawater pushed ashore by wind and low pressure, causes most cyclone deaths. Other hazards are destructive wind, very heavy rain and inland flooding, which can occur hundreds of kilometres from the landfall point.

## Warning stages in India

IMD issues staged bulletins as a system approaches: pre-cyclone watch, cyclone alert, cyclone warning and post-landfall outlook, with increasing lead time detail. Coastal districts operate cyclone shelters and evacuation plans based on these bulletins.

## Preparing

Know whether your area is in an evacuation zone, keep an emergency kit with water, food, medicines, documents and a torch, and keep a battery radio for when networks fail. Evacuate when told to, before conditions deteriorate. The calm during the eye of a cyclone is temporary and is followed by winds from the opposite direction.
