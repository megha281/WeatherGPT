# The UV index

> Source: World Health Organization global solar UV index guidance, public documentation

## What the index means

The UV index describes the strength of ultraviolet radiation at the surface on an open scale starting at zero. It is highest near solar noon, increases with altitude, and is stronger at lower latitudes. Cloud cover reduces it only partly, and snow, sand and water reflect UV, raising exposure.

## Categories and recommended protection

- 0 to 2 (low): no protection needed for most people.
- 3 to 5 (moderate): seek shade near midday, wear a hat and sunglasses, apply sunscreen.
- 6 to 7 (high): protection required; reduce time in the sun between 10:00 and 16:00.
- 8 to 10 (very high): extra protection; unprotected skin can burn quickly.
- 11 and above (extreme): avoid being outdoors during midday hours; take all precautions.

Most of India records very high to extreme values during clear days from March to June, particularly at midday.

## Practical protection

Use a broad-spectrum sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher and reapply every two hours and after sweating or swimming. Tightly woven clothing, a wide-brimmed hat and UV-blocking sunglasses give reliable protection. Shade is most effective in the hours around noon, when UV is at its peak.
