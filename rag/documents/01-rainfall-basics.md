# Rainfall: measurement, categories and what the numbers mean

> Source: India Meteorological Department rainfall classification and WMO observation guidance (public documentation)

## How rainfall is measured

Rainfall is measured as the depth of water that would collect on a flat surface if nothing drained or evaporated, reported in millimetres (mm). One millimetre of rain equals one litre of water per square metre. A rain gauge collects rainfall over a fixed period, usually 24 hours, from 08:30 IST to 08:30 IST in India.

## Daily rainfall categories used in India

The India Meteorological Department groups 24-hour station rainfall into standard categories:

- Very light rain: 0.1 mm to 2.4 mm
- Light rain: 2.5 mm to 15.5 mm
- Moderate rain: 15.6 mm to 64.4 mm
- Heavy rain: 64.5 mm to 115.5 mm
- Very heavy rain: 115.6 mm to 204.4 mm
- Extremely heavy rain: more than 204.4 mm

These thresholds matter because urban drainage systems are usually designed for moderate rainfall. Once totals cross the heavy category, waterlogging and traffic disruption become likely even in well-drained cities.

## Rain probability versus rainfall amount

Probability of precipitation is the chance that measurable rain (at least 0.1 mm) falls at a given point during a given hour or day. It says nothing about intensity. A 90% probability with 1 mm forecast means a near-certain drizzle. A 40% probability with 50 mm forecast means rain is less likely, but heavy if it arrives. Always read the two numbers together.

## Rainfall intensity

Intensity is depth per hour. Short bursts above roughly 10 mm per hour overwhelm most storm drains and are the usual cause of flash urban flooding, even when the daily total looks unremarkable.
