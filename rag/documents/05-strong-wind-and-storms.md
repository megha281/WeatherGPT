# Wind speed, gusts and safety

> Source: Beaufort wind scale (WMO) and general public-safety guidance

## Sustained wind versus gusts

Sustained wind is an average over a period, usually ten minutes. A gust is a brief peak, typically lasting seconds. Damage usually tracks gust speed rather than the average, so forecasts report both.

## What wind speeds feel like

- 20 to 38 km/h: leaves and small branches move; dust is raised.
- 39 to 61 km/h: whole trees sway; walking against the wind is difficult.
- 62 to 88 km/h: twigs break off trees; light structural damage begins; two-wheeler travel becomes risky.
- 89 to 117 km/h: trees uproot, hoardings and roofing sheets fail.
- Above 118 km/h: hurricane force on the Beaufort scale, with widespread damage.

## Practical precautions

Secure or bring in loose items such as flower pots, signage and drying racks. Park away from old trees, scaffolding and advertising hoardings. Close and latch windows on the windward side. On the road, hold the steering wheel firmly when passing gaps between buildings or crossing bridges and flyovers, where crosswinds are strongest.

## Squalls and sudden gusts

A squall is a sudden increase in wind speed lasting several minutes, often just ahead of a thunderstorm. Squall gusts can arrive before any rain, which is why outdoor structures and tents fail unexpectedly at the start of a storm.
