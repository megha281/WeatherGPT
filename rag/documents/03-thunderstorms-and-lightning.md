# Thunderstorms and lightning safety

> Source: WMO and National Disaster Management Authority (India) lightning safety guidance, public documentation

## How thunderstorms form

Thunderstorms need moisture, an unstable atmosphere where air keeps rising, and a trigger such as daytime heating or a front. Rapidly rising moist air builds a cumulonimbus cloud, and charge separation inside the cloud produces lightning. Most single-cell storms last under an hour, but severe storms can persist much longer and produce hail, sudden wind gusts and intense rain.

## The 30/30 rule

If the gap between a lightning flash and its thunder is less than 30 seconds, the strike is within about 10 km and close enough to be dangerous: move indoors immediately. Stay inside for 30 minutes after the last thunder, because strikes often occur at the trailing edge of a storm under apparently clearing skies.

## Safe and unsafe places

Safe: a fully enclosed building with wiring and plumbing, or a hard-topped vehicle with windows closed. Unsafe: open fields, hilltops, beaches, isolated trees, bus shelters, verandahs, metal sheds, and anywhere near tall metal objects.

## Indoors during a storm

Avoid corded phones, plumbing and direct contact with concrete walls or floors that contain metal reinforcement. Unplug sensitive electronics before the storm arrives rather than during it.

## Lightning risk in agriculture

Farm workers in open fields are among the most affected groups in India. Where a storm is forecast, plan field work for earlier in the day and identify a solid shelter before starting.
