# Common weather terminology

> Source: WMO International Meteorological Vocabulary and IMD public glossary

## Terms you will see in forecasts

- Apparent temperature or "feels like": the temperature the body perceives, combining air temperature with humidity and wind.
- Probability of precipitation: the chance of at least 0.1 mm of rain at a point in a stated period.
- Scattered showers: brief rain affecting some places in an area, with dry gaps in between.
- Overcast: cloud covering essentially the whole sky.
- Squall: a sudden increase in wind speed of at least 30 km/h sustained for a few minutes.
- Gust: a brief peak in wind speed lasting seconds.
- Wind direction: always the direction the wind is coming from, so a westerly wind blows from west to east.
- Visibility: the greatest horizontal distance at which objects can be identified; below 1000 m is fog, 1000 m to 5000 m is mist or haze depending on humidity.
- Cloudburst: in Indian usage, rainfall of about 100 mm or more in one hour over a small area.

## Watch, alert and warning

A watch means conditions are favourable for hazardous weather. An alert or advisory means hazardous weather is likely and preparation should begin. A warning means hazardous weather is occurring or imminent and protective action should be taken now. IMD also uses a colour code: green for no action, yellow for be aware, orange for be prepared, and red for take action.
