# Atmospheric pressure and weather systems

> Source: WMO and standard meteorological reference material

## What pressure measures

Atmospheric pressure is the weight of the air column above a point, reported in hectopascals (hPa). Mean sea level pressure averages about 1013 hPa. Values are corrected to sea level so that readings from different altitudes can be compared.

## Highs and lows

In a low pressure area, air converges and rises, cools, and condenses, so lows are associated with clouds, rain and unsettled weather. In a high pressure area, air sinks and warms, which suppresses cloud formation and brings clearer, more settled weather.

## Reading pressure changes

The trend matters more than the absolute value. A drop of 3 hPa or more in three hours often indicates an approaching or deepening system with strengthening wind. A steady rise usually means improving conditions. Rapid deep falls near the coast during the cyclone season are a serious sign.

## Fronts

A front is the boundary between two air masses of different temperature and humidity. Fronts are the main driver of mid-latitude weather changes, producing bands of cloud and rain. In tropical India, day-to-day weather is driven more by the monsoon trough, low pressure systems over the Bay of Bengal, western disturbances in winter, and local convection than by classical fronts.

## Monsoon systems

The southwest monsoon (June to September) and the northeast monsoon (October to December, affecting Tamil Nadu and adjoining regions) deliver most of India's annual rainfall, modulated by monsoon lows, depressions and active or break phases.
