# Using weather information for everyday decisions

> Source: General public-safety guidance; thresholds stated here follow IMD and Beaufort classifications

## Should I carry an umbrella

If the probability of precipitation is above about 40% at the time you will be outside, carrying rain protection is reasonable. Above 70%, plan for rain rather than hoping to avoid it. Check hourly values rather than the daily summary: a daily 60% may all sit in one evening window.

## Is it safe to travel

Consider four things: rainfall intensity during your travel window, gust speed, visibility, and thunderstorm risk. Heavy rain reduces braking grip and visibility; gusts above about 60 km/h affect two-wheelers and high-sided vehicles; visibility below 500 m makes highway driving hazardous; and thunderstorms add lightning risk for anyone outdoors. If two or more of these apply, consider delaying the trip.

## Planning outdoor work or sport

Schedule strenuous activity outside the 11:00 to 16:00 window on hot days, and check the UV index as well as the temperature. On days with afternoon thunderstorm potential, finish outdoor sessions before the usual convective peak in the late afternoon.

## Drying clothes, farming and events

Look at the rainfall probability across consecutive hours, not the daily total, and at humidity, since high humidity slows drying even without rain. For events, gust speed matters for tents and stages, and thunderstorm probability matters for any gathering in the open.
