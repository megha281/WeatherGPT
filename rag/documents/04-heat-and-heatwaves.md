# Heat, heatwaves and heat illness

> Source: India Meteorological Department heatwave criteria and World Health Organization heat-health guidance, public documentation

## How a heatwave is declared in India

IMD declares a heatwave for plains when the maximum temperature reaches at least 40°C and is 4.5°C to 6.4°C above the normal for that place and date; a severe heatwave when the departure exceeds 6.4°C. A heatwave is also declared when the actual maximum reaches 45°C, and a severe heatwave at 47°C, regardless of departure. Thresholds are lower for hilly and coastal areas.

## Why humidity matters

The body cools mainly by evaporating sweat. High humidity slows evaporation, so 36°C with very high humidity can stress the body more than 40°C in dry air. "Feels like" or apparent temperature combines air temperature, humidity and wind to describe this.

## Recognising heat illness

- Heat cramps: painful muscle spasms, heavy sweating.
- Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, weakness, nausea, headache, cool clammy skin, fast weak pulse. Move to a cool place, loosen clothing, sip water, apply cool wet cloths.
- Heat stroke: body temperature above about 40°C, hot skin that may be dry, confusion or unconsciousness. This is a medical emergency. Call for help, move the person to shade and cool them aggressively with water while waiting.

## Reducing risk

Drink water before feeling thirsty, avoid direct sun between 11:00 and 16:00, wear light loose cotton clothing, and reschedule strenuous outdoor work. Infants, people over 65, pregnant women, outdoor workers and people with chronic illness are at higher risk. Never leave children or pets in parked vehicles.
