# Weather versus climate

> Source: WMO and IPCC public explanatory material

## The difference

Weather is the state of the atmosphere over minutes to days: today's temperature, this evening's rain, tomorrow's wind. Climate is the statistical description of weather over long periods, conventionally 30 years, including averages, extremes and variability. A common summary is that climate is what you expect and weather is what you get.

## Climate normals

A climate normal is the average of a variable over a standard 30-year period, currently 1991 to 2020. Normals are the baseline used to say whether a month was warmer or wetter than usual, and they are how departures such as "4.5°C above normal" in heatwave criteria are defined.

## Why a cold day does not disprove warming

Climate statements describe long-term averages across large areas. Individual cold days, or a single wet month, are weather. Trends are assessed over decades and across many stations, and are separated from natural variability such as El Niño and La Niña using statistical methods.

## Climate variability in India

Year-to-year monsoon rainfall in India varies substantially, partly in response to sea surface temperature patterns in the Pacific and Indian Oceans. El Niño years are often, though not always, associated with weaker monsoon rainfall. Because of this variability, a single season is rarely evidence of a long-term trend.

## How climate information is used

Planners use climate statistics for drainage design, crop calendars, reservoir operation and heat action plans, while weather forecasts drive day-to-day decisions and disaster response.
