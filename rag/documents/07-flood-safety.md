# Flood safety

> Source: National Disaster Management Authority (India) flood guidance, public documentation

## Types of flooding

River or riverine flooding builds over hours to days as a catchment drains. Flash flooding develops within minutes to a few hours from intense rain, dam or embankment failure, or cloudbursts, and is the most dangerous because there is little warning. Coastal flooding comes from storm surge and high tides.

## Key rule

Turn around, do not drown. Never enter moving water on foot or in a vehicle. Depth is impossible to judge, roads may be washed away beneath the surface, and water rises faster than most people expect.

## If a flood warning is issued

Move livestock, vehicles and valuables to higher ground early. Switch off electricity and gas at the mains if water is likely to enter. Keep documents, medicines and a phone charger in a grab bag. Follow the evacuation route advised by local authorities rather than a familiar shortcut that may be submerged.

## If trapped by rising water

Move to the highest floor but avoid a sealed attic where you could be trapped by rising water. Signal for help from a window or roof. Keep a whistle and a torch with you.

## Health after a flood

Floodwater mixes with sewage, chemicals and debris. Wash hands before eating, boil drinking water until supply is declared safe, treat cuts promptly, and watch for fever or diarrhoea. Snakebite and electrocution risks rise during cleanup.
