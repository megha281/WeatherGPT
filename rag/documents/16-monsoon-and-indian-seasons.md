# Seasons and the monsoon in India

> Source: India Meteorological Department seasonal definitions, public documentation

## The four meteorological seasons

IMD divides the year into winter (January to February), pre-monsoon or summer (March to May), the southwest monsoon (June to September), and the post-monsoon or northeast monsoon (October to December).

## Southwest monsoon

The southwest monsoon normally reaches Kerala around 1 June and covers the country by mid-July, withdrawing from northwest India from mid-September. It delivers roughly three quarters of India's annual rainfall. Within the season, active spells with widespread rain alternate with break spells that can last a week or more.

## Northeast monsoon

From October to December, retreating winds bring rainfall to Tamil Nadu, coastal Andhra Pradesh, Rayalaseema, south interior Karnataka and Kerala. For Tamil Nadu this is the main rainy season, and it coincides with the peak cyclone period in the Bay of Bengal.

## Pre-monsoon severe weather

March to May brings intense surface heating, heatwaves over central and northern India, and localised severe thunderstorms with hail and squalls. In eastern India these are known as Nor'westers or Kalbaisakhi and can produce damaging wind gusts with little warning.

## Regional note: interior Karnataka

Districts of interior Karnataka such as Ballari (Bellary) are semi-arid, with hot dry summers, moderate southwest monsoon rain and a secondary rainfall peak in September and October. Rainfall there is more variable year to year than on the coast, which makes the forecast probability more useful than any single seasonal average.
